Guide

An AI field guide that wants you to put your phone away

Pocket Field Guide plans short walks, names the plants and animals you find, and keeps a private journal. Both AI models run inside your browser, so it works on a trail with no signal and never uploads anything.

Quick start

Use Chrome or Edge on a laptop or desktop: they support WebGPU, which makes Gemma fast. Other browsers may work but haven't been tested.

  1. Download the models once

    Open Setup (or the "Get started" card) and click Download models. It's about 900 MB, so use Wi-Fi. This happens only once, and the app stays usable while it downloads.

  2. Plan a walk

    On Go outside, pick a time and a place and press Plan my walk. You get 3–5 things to look for in about ten seconds.

  3. Walk, spot, identify

    Press Start walking. The screen turns into a timer and checklist. When something catches your eye, tap I found something, take one photo and save it to your journal.

Install it like an app. In Chrome, use the install icon in the address bar (or "Add to Home screen" on a phone) and it opens in its own window, even offline.

Going for a walk

The walk planner is the heart of the app. It gives you a reason to go out and things to notice once you're there.

How long?
15 minutes gives you 3 things to find, 30 minutes gives 4, and an hour gives 5.
Where?
Streets, park, woods, garden or water. The list changes to match: city walks get cracks in pavement and rooftop birds, woods get bark, moss and tracks.
Season
Worked out from today's date, so autumn lists include falling leaves and seeds. If you've saved a location south of the equator, the seasons flip.
Variety
Things you've already saved to your journal are left out of new lists. Don't like a list? Press Give me different ones.

Walking mode

Once you press Start walking, the app hides everything except the timer and your checklist. That's on purpose: the less there is to look at, the more you look around.

  • Tick items off as you find them. The counter shows how many are left.
  • The timer can run over. It counts up past zero in green. Nobody is rushing you home.
  • Closed the tab by accident? Your walk resumes where you left off.
  • End walk saves your minutes outside and how many you found to the journal stats.

Identifying a find

Use Identify or the I found something button during a walk. You can drop a photo in, paste one (Ctrl+V), choose a file, or use your webcam.

Taking a photo that works

✓ Do
  • Fill the frame with one subject
  • Show the parts that matter: a bird's face and breast, a mushroom's cap, a whole leaf
  • Keep the sun behind you
✗ Avoid
  • Wide shots of a whole hedge or garden
  • Heavy digital zoom and blur
  • Strong backlight that turns it into a silhouette

Reading the result

You get a name, a group (bird, tree, flower, bug, mushroom...) and a confidence bar.

Confident
60% or more. Usually right for common species. Still use the field note to check.
Likely
30–60%. Look at the other options too: similar species often score close together.
Unsure
Below 30%. Either it isn't in the field guide yet or the photo is hard. Try a closer, clearer shot.

Not right? Tap one of the other options under the name. The field note rewrites itself for that species, and your choice is saved as "you confirmed".

The field guide covers about 150 common species across North America, Europe and South Asia: garden birds, common trees, wildflowers, well-known mushrooms and insects. It always picks the closest match it knows, so a rare species may come back as a similar common one. See add your own species.

Reading a field note

After every identification, Gemma writes a short note. It isn't an encyclopedia entry. It's a nudge to look more closely and keep exploring.

Check it: Look for a pinkish-orange body and black-and-white striped wings.

Look nearby: Scan the lawn for small holes where it has been probing for insects.

Careful: Keep your distance, especially in spring when there may be a nest nearby.

Check it
Two features to confirm in person. This is how you catch a wrong ID: if the real thing doesn't match, pick another option.
Look nearby
One related thing to go and find next, which keeps the walk going.
Careful
A safety or respect tip. These come from a fixed, hand-written list, never from the AI.

Why you can trust it more than a chatbot

Small AI models sound confident even when they're wrong. So Gemma is never asked to remember facts. Each species in the field guide has a line of hand-written ID traits, and Gemma only rephrases those into things to check. Every note is then checked automatically. If it leaves out a section, drifts from the trusted traits or repeats the instructions, the app replaces it with a plain note built straight from the traits.

Never eat anything because an app identified it. That goes double for mushrooms, where deadly and edible species can look almost the same. Use this app to notice and learn, not to forage.

Your journal

Every find you save goes into Journal with its photo (as a small thumbnail), the time, your own note and the field note. The stats at the top count your minutes outside, walks, finds and different species.

  • Location is opt-in. Tick "Save the location with this find" and the browser asks for permission once. Coordinates are rounded to about 10 m and stored only on this device.
  • Export as Markdown downloads your whole journal as a text file you can keep, print or paste into a notes app.
  • Delete removes a find for good.
  • The journal lives in this browser. Clearing site data, or using a private window, erases it, so export it now and then.

Using it offline

After the first download, the app, both models and your journal are all stored on your device.

Check it really works offline

  1. Open Setup and wait until both models say Ready, saved for offline use.
  2. The status pill at the top should read Ready, works offline.
  3. Turn on airplane mode (or disconnect Wi-Fi) and reload the page. It should load and the pill should read Offline and ready.
  4. Identify a photo. If it works, you're set for the trail.

What the status pill means

Models not downloaded
Nothing is downloaded yet. Open Setup.
Loading models…
Downloading, or loading from your device (about 15–20 s on later visits).
Ready, works offline
Everything is stored locally.
Ready (online only)
The models work but couldn't be saved, usually because the browser is low on storage. See troubleshooting.

Settings

Model size
1B (default, ~785 MB) writes clearly better notes and lists. 270M (~300 MB) is smaller and faster, but its writing is weaker, so more notes fall back to the plain template.
Run on: Auto
Uses your graphics chip (WebGPU) when the browser supports it. If that fails, it switches to the CPU by itself and remembers that for next time.
Run on: GPU / CPU
Forces one or the other. The CPU always uses the 270M model, because the 4-bit 1B model can't run on the browser's CPU backend.
Remove models
Deletes the downloaded models to free space. Your journal is kept.

Typical speed on a mid-range laptop (Intel Iris Xe): identifying a photo takes 1–2 s, and a field note takes about 10 s on the GPU.

Troubleshooting

The download is stuck or very slow

The Gemma file is large (~785 MB). Keep the tab open and on Wi-Fi. If it stops, reload the page. Finished files are kept, so it picks up where it left off.

It says "Ready (online only)"

Your browser didn't let the site store ~900 MB. Free some disk space, leave private/incognito mode, and make sure the site isn't blocked from storing data. Then use Remove models and download again. Chrome and Edge normally allow several GB.

Field notes are slow

Check Setup → Your device. If WebGPU shows as "not available", Gemma is running on the CPU. Use Chrome or Edge and update your graphics drivers. Choosing the 270M model also speeds things up.

"GPU unavailable for Gemma, switching to CPU"

Your graphics driver couldn't run the model, so the app switched to the CPU automatically. To try the GPU again later, choose Run on: Auto in Setup, which clears the remembered failure.

The identification is wrong

Look at the other options first; the right one is often second. Then try a closer, clearer photo of the key parts. If the species isn't in the field guide at all, the app can't name it (yet).

The camera button doesn't work

The browser needs camera permission, and the site must be opened over https (or localhost). If you'd rather not allow it, take the photo with your camera app and use Choose a photo.

My journal disappeared

The journal is stored in this browser only. A different browser, a private window, or clearing site data will show an empty journal. Export it regularly to keep a copy.

Privacy

Nothing you do in the app leaves your device: no photos, notes, locations or journal entries. There is no account, analytics or server.

  • The only network requests are loading the app itself and downloading the model files from Hugging Face the first time.
  • You can check this yourself: open your browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab, and identify a photo. Nothing is sent.
  • Data is stored in the browser's own storage for this site (IndexedDB for the journal, the Origin Private File System for models). Removing the site's data in your browser settings erases all of it.

Add your own species

The field guide is a plain list in src/labels.js. Each entry has a name, a group and a line of ID traits:

['hoopoe', 'bird', 'pinkish-orange body; black-and-white striped wings; fan-like crest tipped black; long curved bill'],
  1. Add your local species with traits a beginner can check by eye.
  2. Run npm run embed to update the field guide's embeddings (a minute or two).
  3. Run npm run build and redeploy, or use npm run dev locally.

No training or fine-tuning: SigLIP recognises the new names right away, and Gemma uses your traits in its notes.

How it works

👁️

SigLIP sees

Google's open image–text model compares your photo with a description of every species in the field guide and ranks the closest matches. The descriptions are processed ahead of time, so only the image half of the model (~100 MB) runs on your device.

✍️

Gemma writes

Google's open-weight Gemma 3 language model turns trusted traits into field notes and plans your walks. It runs on your graphics chip through WebGPU, or on the CPU as a fallback.

💾

Your browser stores

Models are saved to the browser's private file system, the app is cached by a service worker, and your journal lives in IndexedDB. Together they make the app work fully offline.

Built with Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web. Models: Gemma 3 1B (Gemma Terms of Use), SigLIP base (Apache-2.0). Source code is MIT-licensed.
